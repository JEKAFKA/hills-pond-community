Lake FAQ

Common questions about Hills Pond: the water, the rules of the road, and how to enjoy it as a good neighbor.

The answers below were gathered from public records and general regional information. A few items (noted as such) should be confirmed with the New York State DEC or the Town of Hampton before being relied on.

Where exactly is Hills Pond?

Hills Pond is in the town of Hampton, in the far northeastern corner of Washington County, New York (ZIP code 12887). It sits just west of Crystal Lake and roughly 6.8 miles from the village of Whitehall. Its approximate coordinates are 43.5144° N, 73.2801° W, and Hills Pond Road runs along the shoreline.

How big is the lake?

Hills Pond covers about 18 acres. It is a small, quiet lake, easy to paddle across, and intimate enough that most of the shoreline households know one another.

Maximum depth has not been confirmed from an official source; check with the NYS DEC for a depth/contour map if needed.

What are the boating rules?

Gas-powered motors are not permitted on Hills Pond. Boating is limited to non-gas craft: canoes, kayaks, paddleboards, rowboats, and electric trolling motors. This restriction is a big part of why the lake stays calm, clean, and quiet all year.

Please give swimmers, anglers, and wildlife plenty of room, and keep noise to a minimum out of respect for neighboring households.

Can I fish in Hills Pond? What might I catch?

Yes. Lakes of this size and character in Washington County commonly hold warm-water species such as largemouth bass, yellow perch, sunfish, and bullhead, and some area waters also support trout.

A valid New York State freshwater fishing license is required for anyone 16 and older. Always follow current NYS DEC seasons, size, and catch limits.

The exact species present in Hills Pond have not been confirmed from an official stocking/survey record, so please verify with the NYS DEC. Licenses are available at dec.ny.gov.

Is swimming allowed?

There is no public beach or lifeguard at Hills Pond. It is a private/residential lake, so swimming is generally from residents' own shoreline and is at your own risk. Watch out for changing depths, weeds, and cold spots, and never let children swim unsupervised.

What wildlife and plants will I see?

A quiet, motor-light lake like this is a haven for wildlife. Expect to see waterfowl such as ducks and geese, herons, turtles, frogs, and the occasional beaver or muskrat, along with songbirds and deer in the surrounding woods. Lily pads and shoreline reeds are part of a healthy lake ecosystem, so please avoid disturbing them.

How can we keep the water clean and healthy?

A few neighborly habits go a long way:

• Avoid fertilizers, herbicides, and pesticides near the shoreline, since nutrients run off into the lake and feed algae.
• Maintain a buffer of natural plants along the bank.
• Keep septic systems in good working order.
• Clean, drain, and dry any boats or gear brought from other waters to prevent invasive species.
• Carry out all trash and fishing line.

Is it safe to walk or skate on the ice in winter?

Ice is never guaranteed to be safe. Thickness varies across the lake due to springs, inflows, and currents. As a general guideline, the NYS DEC suggests at least 4 inches of clear, solid ice for walking and more for groups or vehicles, but always test conditions yourself and never go out alone. When in doubt, stay off.

A little about the town of Hampton

The town of Hampton was formed in 1786 and sits in the northeastern corner of Washington County, sharing its eastern border with Vermont along the Poultney River. It is a small rural community, home to fewer than a thousand residents, with deep roots dating back to settlers from Massachusetts and Connecticut.
